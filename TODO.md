# Website plan and content conventions

This file tracks the agreed direction for Computing Christianity. Keep it current as decisions are made.

## Site structure

- Case for Christianity: focused positive-case resources on God's existence, Jesus and the resurrection, and the Bible.
- Christian Life: Christian beliefs and practices, including Bible reading and study, prayer, memorization, serving, and mentoring.
- Apologetics & Conversations: Christian apologetics skills and tactics, conversation guides, and defensive apologetics resources. Historical evidence for Christian beliefs belongs under Defensive Apologetics; this resource may use Scripture where relevant but will not cover philosophical evidence.
- Other Religions: resources for understanding a religion generally, engaging its claims from a Christian perspective, and serving people who are part of that religion.
- About: founders, the ministry, other ministries and projects, and support.

## Resource page pattern

- Start with the question, intended audience, and a brief overview.
- Present one focused, organized resource for a major topic rather than many disconnected short articles.
- Use the existing evidence-for-God page as the current example to learn from when shaping other major resources.
- Organize the explanation into clear sections, then include relevant objections or questions, practical application where appropriate, and related resources.
- Credit and link books, articles, primary sources, and videos. Keep research notes distinct from sourced conclusions.
- Add an "Any Questions?" searchable index after there is enough content to make it useful.
- Use the existing Astro/HTML and MDX patterns and site styles. Preserve existing `.html` URLs.
