<h1 className="my-h1">What Is Christianity?</h1>

This guide is for people who want to understand the core or central beliefs that Christians have and see the ***biblical*** basis for those beliefs. 

Its focus is Scripture; philosophical, scientific, and historical arguments can be explored in other related resources. 
  
If you are Christian, you might use this page to see where in the Bible / Scripture our beliefs are taught. 

Also, this is not meant to be an exhaustive list of all the Scriptures where a certain belief is taught.

<ContentBox tone="orange">
  <p><strong>Note:</strong> There are many beliefs that differ among Christians, thus many denominations. Those are likely not listed here. 
  This page is meant to cover the CORE beliefs of Christianity which
  are taught clearly in the Bible. This is not to say perfect understanding of each of these doctrines are necessary to be Christian, they are however, foundational
  to the Christian faith.</p>
</ContentBox>
<h2 id="at-a-glance">At a glance</h2>


The basic overview and timeline of Christianity is this:

God makes all things (**Creation**). Mankind fails and sins (**The Fall**). Jesus Christ comes
to die on our behalf for our sins (**Redemption**). Jesus rises from the dead, proving and 
promising victory over death for all who belief in Him (**Resurrection**). Jesus will one
day come back for judgement, and we will all have to face the consequences of all our
actions (**2nd Coming of Christ**). And finally, we will all either live with God, or be
cast into hell for eternity (**Eternity**).


<p >These events form one connected story: the God who created humanity addresses the problem of sin by acting to save through Jesus. The resurrection shows that death does not have the final word, and Christian hope looks toward life with God. The sections below unpack these claims and point to the biblical passages Christians use to support them.</p>

<h2 id="god" className="numbered">God</h2>

<h3 id="identity-and-nature" className="numbered">Identity and Nature: Who is God?</h3>
There is one God, who exists as 3 persons. He created everything, and created people to be in His image. He is eternal, self-existent, unchanging, and worthy of worship.
- **God is one (monotheism):** There is only one God. (<ScriptureReferences citations="Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 45:5–6" />).
- **Trinity:** God exists as one being in 3 persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. <ScriptureReferences citations="Matthew 3:16–17; Matthew 28:19" />.
- **Creator of everything:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:1; Colossians 1:16–17" />.
- **Eternal:** God has no beginning and no end.  <ScriptureReferences citations="Revelation 22:13" />.
- **Self-Existent (Divine Aseity):** God depends on nothing outside Himself for His existence. <ScriptureReferences citations="Exodus 3:14" />.
- **Unchanging:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Malachi 3:6; Hebrews 13:8" />.
- **Spirit:** He is spirit and not a physical creature. <ScriptureReferences citations="John 4:24" />.
- **Worthy of worship:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Revelation 4:11; Matthew 4:10" />.

<h3 id="power-and-knowledge" className="numbered">Power and knowledge: What is God capable of?</h3>
God is omnipotent, omniscient, and omnipresent.
- **All-powerful (omnipotent):** God can do anything <ScriptureReferences citations="Jeremiah 32:17; Matthew 19:26" />.
- **All-knowing (omniscient):** God knows everything, and also has perfect wisdom <ScriptureReferences citations="Psalm 139:1–6; Romans 11:33–36" />.
- **Present everywhere (omnipresent):** <ScriptureReferences citations="Psalm 139:7–10; Jeremiah 23:23–24" />.

<h3 id="gods-moral-character" className="numbered">God's moral character: What is God like?</h3>
God is holy, righteous, perfect, good, loving, and faithful in His very nature. He loves us.
- **Holy and perfect:** God is righteous, morally good, has no flaw. <ScriptureReferences citations="Isaiah 6:3; Matthew 5:48; 1 Peter 1:15–16" />.
- **Faithful:** God stays the same, and never lies. <ScriptureReferences citations="Deuteronomy 7:9; 2 Timothy 2:13" />.
- **Loving:** God is love.<ScriptureReferences citations="John 3:16; 1 John 4:8–10" />
- **Just:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Deuteronomy 32:4; Psalm 89:14" />.
- **Merciful:** God desires to show mercy<ScriptureReferences citations="Exodus 34:6–7; Ephesians 2:4–5" />.

<h3 id="gods-roles" className="numbered">God's roles</h3>

Scripture describes God through His relationship to creation and His people:

- **Creator:** God has made all things. <ScriptureReferences citations="Nehemiah 9:6; Revelation 4:11" />.
- **Sustainer:** God holds all things together. <ScriptureReferences citations="Colossians 1:17" />.
- **Ruler and King:** God is King of the universe. He's in charge. <ScriptureReferences citations="Psalm 103:19; 1 Timothy 6:15" />.
- **Lawgiver and Judge:** God is the one who sets & enforces the rules. <ScriptureReferences citations="James 4:12; Psalm 9:7–8" />.
- **Savior and Redeemer:** God saves us from our sins. <ScriptureReferences citations="Isaiah 43:11; Ephesians 1:7" />.
- **Father:** God is our Father, He made us. <ScriptureReferences citations="Matthew 6:9; 2 Corinthians 6:18" />.
- **Provider:** God is the provider of our needs. <ScriptureReferences citations="Matthew 6:25–33; James 1:17" />.

<p >These roles fit together: God creates and sustains all things, rules as king and judge, and relates to His people as savior, father, and provider. This helps show that Scripture describes not only what God is like, but also how He acts toward creation and people.</p>

<h2 id="creation-and-humanity" className="numbered">Creation and Humanity</h2>

<h3 id="creation-from-nothing" className="numbered">Creation From Nothing</h3>

Creation ***"ex nihilo"*** means that God created all things without using pre-existing matter.

**References:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:1; John 1:3; Hebrews 11:3" />.

<h3 id="image-of-god" className="numbered">The Image of God</h3>

God created human beings in His own image. A technical term for this is ***"imago dei"*** (Latin for "image of God").

**References:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:26–27; Genesis 9:6; James 3:9" />.

<h3 id="falleness-of-man" className="numbered">Falleness of Humanity</h3>
Adam and Eve, the first two people, disobeyed God and did the first sin.
This resulted in humanity becoming "fallen", even though originally God made
all things "good" (<ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:31"/>). We typically say they have a "sin nature" or are sinful. 
There is some variety on what this means, but it includes people's natural desire to sin, be selfish, steal, lie, etc.

A good example of this is how children seem to naturally hide things when they did wrong. You typically 
have to teach children how to be honest, because they naturally lie. Same with sharing, we naturally want
things for just ourselves, that's why we teach children to share.

**References:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 6:5; Genesis 8:21; Jeremiah 17:9" />.

<h3 id="sin-and-consequences" className="numbered">Sin and its Consequences</h3>

Sin is doing evil. Earlier we saw how God is holy, perfect, good, just, and also the ultimate Judge ([see "God" section above](./doctrines.html#god)).

Sin corrupted God's perfect and good creation, and since God is perfect it also separates all humanity from Him.

That separation from God, who is life itself, means death.

To see more about the consequences of sin look at "Salvation".

**Starting references:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Romans 3:23; Romans 6:23; Ephesians 2:1–3" />.
