<h1 className="my-h1">What Is Christianity?</h1>

This guide is for people who want to understand the core or central beliefs that Christians have and see the ***biblical*** basis for those beliefs. 

Its focus is Scripture; philosophical, scientific, and historical arguments can be explored in other related resources. 
  
If you are Christian, you might use this page to see where in the Bible / Scripture our beliefs are taught. 

Also, this is not meant to be an exhaustive list of all the Scriptures where a certain belief is taught.

<ContentBox tone="orange">
  <p><strong>Note:</strong> There are many beliefs that differ among Christians, thus many denominations. Those are likely not listed here. 
  This page is meant to cover the CORE beliefs of Christianity which
  are taught clearly in the Bible. This is not to say perfect understanding of each of these doctrines are necessary to be Christian, they are however, foundational
  to the Christian faith.</p>
</ContentBox>
<h2 id="at-a-glance">At a glance</h2>


The basic overview and timeline of Christianity is this:

God makes all things (**Creation**). Mankind fails and sins (**The Fall**). Jesus Christ comes
to die on our behalf for our sins (**Redemption**). Jesus rises from the dead, proving and 
promising victory over death for all who belief in Him (**Resurrection**). Jesus will one
day come back for judgement, and we will all have to face the consequences of all our
actions (**2nd Coming of Christ**). And finally, we will all either live with God, or be
cast into hell for eternity (**Eternity**).


<p >These events form one connected story: the God who created humanity addresses the problem of sin by acting to save through Jesus. The resurrection shows that death does not have the final word, and Christian hope looks toward life with God. The sections below unpack these claims and point to the biblical passages Christians use to support them.</p>

<h2 id="god" className="numbered">God</h2>

<h3 id="identity-and-nature" className="numbered">Identity and Nature: Who is God?</h3>
There is one God, who exists as 3 persons. He created everything, and created people to be in His image. He is eternal, self-existent, unchanging, and worthy of worship.
- **God is one (monotheism):** There is only one God. (<ScriptureReferences citations="Deuteronomy 6:4; Isaiah 45:5–6" />).
- **Trinity:** God exists as one being in 3 persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. <ScriptureReferences citations="Matthew 3:16–17; Matthew 28:19" />.
- **Creator of everything:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:1; Colossians 1:16–17" />.
- **Eternal:** God has no beginning and no end.  <ScriptureReferences citations="Revelation 22:13" />.
- **Self-Existent (Divine Aseity):** God depends on nothing outside Himself for His existence. <ScriptureReferences citations="Exodus 3:14" />.
- **Unchanging:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Malachi 3:6; Hebrews 13:8" />.
- **Spirit:** He is spirit and not a physical creature. <ScriptureReferences citations="John 4:24" />.
- **Worthy of worship:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Revelation 4:11; Matthew 4:10" />.

<h3 id="power-and-knowledge" className="numbered">Power and knowledge: What is God capable of?</h3>
God is omnipotent, omniscient, and omnipresent.
- **All-powerful (omnipotent):** God can do anything <ScriptureReferences citations="Jeremiah 32:17; Matthew 19:26" />.
- **All-knowing (omniscient):** God knows everything, and also has perfect wisdom <ScriptureReferences citations="Psalm 139:1–6; Romans 11:33–36" />.
- **Present everywhere (omnipresent):** <ScriptureReferences citations="Psalm 139:7–10; Jeremiah 23:23–24" />.

<h3 id="gods-moral-character" className="numbered">God's moral character: What is God like?</h3>
God is holy, righteous, perfect, good, loving, and faithful in His very nature. He loves us.
- **Holy and perfect:** God is righteous, morally good, has no flaw. <ScriptureReferences citations="Isaiah 6:3; Matthew 5:48; 1 Peter 1:15–16" />.
- **Faithful:** God stays the same, and never lies. <ScriptureReferences citations="Deuteronomy 7:9; 2 Timothy 2:13" />.
- **Loving:** God is love.<ScriptureReferences citations="John 3:16; 1 John 4:8–10" />
- **Just:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Deuteronomy 32:4; Psalm 89:14" />.
- **Merciful:** God desires to show mercy<ScriptureReferences citations="Exodus 34:6–7; Ephesians 2:4–5" />.

<h3 id="gods-roles" className="numbered">God's roles</h3>

Scripture describes God through His relationship to creation and His people:

- **Creator:** God has made all things. <ScriptureReferences citations="Nehemiah 9:6; Revelation 4:11" />.
- **Sustainer:** God holds all things together. <ScriptureReferences citations="Colossians 1:17" />.
- **Ruler and King:** God is King of the universe. He's in charge. <ScriptureReferences citations="Psalm 103:19; 1 Timothy 6:15" />.
- **Lawgiver and Judge:** God is the one who sets & enforces the rules. <ScriptureReferences citations="James 4:12; Psalm 9:7–8" />.
- **Savior and Redeemer:** God saves us from our sins. <ScriptureReferences citations="Isaiah 43:11; Ephesians 1:7" />.
- **Father:** God is our Father, He made us. <ScriptureReferences citations="Matthew 6:9; 2 Corinthians 6:18" />.
- **Provider:** God is the provider of our needs. <ScriptureReferences citations="Matthew 6:25–33; James 1:17" />.

<p >These roles fit together: God creates and sustains all things, rules as king and judge, and relates to His people as savior, father, and provider. This helps show that Scripture describes not only what God is like, but also how He acts toward creation and people.</p>

<h2 id="creation-and-humanity" className="numbered">Creation and Humanity</h2>

<h3 id="creation-from-nothing" className="numbered">Creation From Nothing</h3>

Creation ***"ex nihilo"*** means that God created all things without using pre-existing matter.

**References:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:1; John 1:3; Hebrews 11:3" />.

<h3 id="image-of-god" className="numbered">The Image of God</h3>

God created human beings in His own image. A technical term for this is ***"imago dei"*** (Latin for "image of God").

**References:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:26–27; Genesis 9:6; James 3:9" />.

<h3 id="falleness-of-man" className="numbered">Falleness of Humanity</h3>
Adam and Eve, the first two people, disobeyed God and did the first sin.
This resulted in humanity becoming "fallen", even though originally God made
all things "good" (<ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 1:31"/>). We typically say they have a "sin nature" or are sinful. 
There is some variety on what this means, but it includes people's natural desire to sin, be selfish, steal, lie, etc.

A good example of this is how children seem to naturally hide things when they did wrong. You typically 
have to teach children how to be honest, because they naturally lie. Same with sharing, we naturally want
things for just ourselves, that's why we teach children to share.

**References:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Genesis 6:5; Genesis 8:21; Jeremiah 17:9" />.

<h3 id="sin-and-consequences" className="numbered">Sin and its Consequences</h3>

Sin is doing evil. Earlier we saw how God is holy, perfect, good, just, and also the ultimate Judge ([see "God" section above](./doctrines.html#god)).

Sin corrupted God's perfect and good creation, and since God is perfect it also separates all humanity from Him.

That separation from God, who is life itself, means death.

To see more about the consequences of sin look at "Salvation".

**Starting references:** <ScriptureReferences citations="Romans 3:23; Romans 6:23; Ephesians 2:1–3" />.

<details className="title-disclosure">

Jesus also did this by regularly calling God ***"My Father"***

<details className="title-disclosure">
<summary><strong>"Son of Man"</strong> — Jesus' recurring title for himself, with echoes of Daniel's vision.</summary>

This is Jesus' favorite term for Himself, with over [80 occurences in the Gospels](https://www.blueletterbible.org/search/search.cfm?Criteria=%22son+of+man%22&t=ESV&csr=10#s=s_primary_0_1). 

People sometimes think this just references Jesus' humanity. It normal biblical usage, "son of man" does mean a human, however, Jesus actually uses it to refer to His divinity.

In the Old Testament in <ScriptureReferences citations="Daniel 7:13–14" />, there is a "Son of Man" figure that Daniel sees in a vision. This figure comes on the clouds, ***approaches*** God ,
and God gives ***him*** an eternal glory, dominion, and kingdom, so that all people may ***serve him***.

This is very odd when you consider a few things:
- How the prophet Isaiah responded to seeing God's glory (<ScriptureReferences citations="Isaiah 6:5" />)
- That the Son of Man is given glory, dominion, and a kingdom - things we have noted are God's - [see "God" section above](./doctrines.html#god).
- And all people serve this Son of Man figure, even though we're taught to only serve God. (<ScriptureReferences citations="Deuteronomy 6:13; Matthew 4:10" />)

Jesus claiming to be the Son of Man is actually the last thing He says to the high priest before being crucified, and Jesus is accused of blasphemy.
 (the high priest also asked him if he was the "Christ" and "Son of God")<ScriptureReferences citations="Mark 14:61–64; Luke 22:69–71" />

</details>

<details className="title-disclosure">
<summary><strong>"Messiah" / "Christ"</strong> - both mean “Anointed One.”</summary>

"Messiah" in Hebrew literally means "anointed one". In the Old Testament very special figures were anointed (had oil poured on their head), like kings, and this was symbolic of God 
choosing them etc.

Messiah came to be a term for a specific special indiviudal, where many Old Testament passages actually reference and talk about.
So Messiah became a figure, ***the anointed one***, one specific chosen individual. 

"Christ" is simply the Greek term for Messiah.

So when someone says "Jesus Christ", they are really saying, "Jesus the Messiah", as in Jesus is this special individual prophecied about for hundreds of years.

"Son of" can just mean descendant of. Jews commonly refer to themselves as "sons of Abraham". 

The Messiah is also called "Son of David" because it was prophecied that the Messiah would be a descendant of King David.

<h2 id="salvation-and-the-gospel" className="numbered">The Gospel and Salvation</h2>
"Gospel" comes from the Greek word "<Tooltip id="euangelion-lexicon" className=""><span className="tooltip-prompt">εὐαγγέλιον</span><span slot="tooltip"><a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g2098/nasb95/mgnt/0-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">View in lexicon</a></span></Tooltip>" (euangelion), meaning "good news."

But before we understand the good news of salvation, we have to know what we need saving from ("the bad news") first.

Here's a simple breakdown that we'll expand on in the next section:
- **Bad news:** Sin separates people from God and brings death and judgment. <ScriptureReferences citations="Romans 3:23; Romans 6:23; Ephesians 2:1–3" />.
- **Good news (gospel):** Jesus died for our sins and was raised; forgiveness and new life are offered through Him. <ScriptureReferences citations="1 Corinthians 15:3–4; Romans 3:21–26; Ephesians 2:4–10" />.


<h3 id="the-bad-news-and-the-good-news" className="numbered">The Bad News and the Good News</h3>
##### <span className="">Bad News</span>
Do you believe people who commit moral crimes deserve to be punished? Have you committed any moral crimes? I definitely have, and that's bad news, because
that means I deserve to be punished for all the wrong things I've ever done.


And we can't "work our way out" either. And we know this. If a doctor murders 10 people, a just judge would never let him go free just because the doctor had saved 1,000 people in the past.

You cannot weigh good deeds vs bad deeds.

##### <span className="">Good News</span>
The good news is this, Jesus took your punishment for you.

All of it. (<ScriptureReferences citations="1 John 2:2; Isaiah 53:5–6; 1 Peter 2:24; 2 Corinthians 5:21" />)

And He offers the forgiveness of your sins through His sacrifice. <span className=""><ScriptureReferences citations="Acts 10:43; Ephesians 1:7; Romans 3:24–26" /></span>



<h3 id="receiving-salvation-and-its-effects" className="numbered ">Receiving Salvation and Its Effects</h3>
#### <span className="">How to Receive</span>
There are a few aspects of accepting Jesus' offer of salvation in Scripture. There may be denominational differences about some of these, but they are all taught.

<span className="">Scriptural Examples of Responding to Christ:</span>
- <Tooltip id="repent-term" className="strong-term"><span className="tooltip-prompt"><strong>Repent</strong></span>
<span slot="tooltip" className="tooltip-bullet">Greek: μετανοέω (metanoeō, “meh-tah-no-EH-oh”).</span><span slot="tooltip" className="tooltip-bullet"><a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g3340/esv/tr/0-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">View in lexicon</a></span></Tooltip>: 
A heart / mind change away from sin and toward God. Similar to modern day being "sorry" for what you've done. <span className=""><ScriptureReferences citations="Mark 1:15; Acts 3:19" /></span>.
- **Confess**: There are two parts to this one. Confessing sin, and confessing that Jesus is Lord.
  - Confessing sin: You admit that you've committed sin and need forgiveness. <span className=""><ScriptureReferences citations="1 John 1:9; Proverbs 28:13" /></span>.
  - Confessing Jesus is Lord: You declare Jesus is King of your life and in charge, and that you will follow Him. <span className=""><ScriptureReferences citations="Romans 10:9–10; Matthew 10:32–33" /></span>.
- **Believe** or **Faith**: You need to believe that Jesus is who He says He is (<span className=""><a href="./doctrines.html#jesus-identity-and-nature">see Jesus' Identity</a></span>). 
This includes that He is 
  - the Christ, the Son of God (<ScriptureReferences citations="John 20:31; 1 John 5:1; 1 John 2:22–23; 1 John 5:5" />), 
  - that God raised Him from the dead (<ScriptureReferences citations="Romans 10:9; John 20:24–29" />), 
  - that He died for your sins, rose again, and is coming back (<ScriptureReferences citations="Romans 10:9–10; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4; John 14:3" />), 
  - and that He will give you eternal life (<ScriptureReferences citations="John 3:16; Romans 6:23; 1 John 5:11–13" />).
- <Tooltip id="baptism-term" className="strong-term"><span className="tooltip-prompt"><strong>Be baptized</strong></span><span slot="tooltip" className="tooltip-bullet">Greek: βαπτίζω (baptizō, “bap-TID-zo”).</span><span slot="tooltip" className="tooltip-bullet"><a href="https://www.blueletterbible.org/lexicon/g907/esv/mgnt/0-1/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">View in lexicon</a></span></Tooltip>: To be baptized physically is to immersed in water. 
You do this as an act of faith and a symbol of your new life in Christ. 
  - Baptism in a way is also confessing your sins. John the Baptist proclaimed a baptism "of repentance for the forgiveness of sins" <span className=""><ScriptureReferences citations="Mark 1:4; Luke 3:3" /></span>. 
  - It represents dying and being buried with Christ, then being raised in new life. <span className=""><ScriptureReferences citations="Romans 6:3–11; Colossians 2:12" /></span>.
